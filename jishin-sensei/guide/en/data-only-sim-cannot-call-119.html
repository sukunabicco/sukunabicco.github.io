Your travel SIM can't call 119: how to reach help in Japan

Most travel SIMs and eSIMs are data-only, so the emergency numbers will not connect. Here is what works instead — and what to say when you get through.

⚠️ Data-only SIMs and eSIMs cannot call these numbers. Borrow a phone, or use a public payphone — emergency calls from payphones are free.

📶 Someone collapses right in front of you. You dial 119 — but the call won't connect: your travel eSIM is data-only.

How do you reach 119?

✅ The right move: Ask a passerby, a shop or your hotel to call — or use a public payphone, where emergency calls are free

Why: Most travel SIMs and eSIMs are data-only: they have no voice line, so they cannot call 110, 119 or 118 at all — no amount of redialing helps. Borrow a phone from someone nearby, or find a green public payphone: lift the receiver, press the red emergency button if there is one, and dial — emergency calls need no coins or cards. Check whether your plan includes voice before you travel.

Source: NTT East — Public payphone guide (Japanese page)

☎️ How to use a public payphone

Emergency calls from green public payphones are free.

  1. Lift the receiver
  2. Press the red emergency button if there is one (no button? just dial)
  3. Dial 110 / 119 / 118 — no coins or cards needed

Payphones are powered by the phone line itself, so they keep working during a blackout. Public payphone guide — NTT East (Japanese page)

📞 Emergency calls

  • 110 — Police. Crimes and traffic accidents. Can't speak Japanese? Don't hang up — police can bring an interpreter into a three-way call
  • 119 — Fire & Ambulance. Fires, serious injury or illness. Many dispatch centers can arrange interpretation — stay on the line
  • 118 — Coast Guard. Emergencies at sea
  • 050-3816-2787 — Japan Visitor Hotline (JNTO). 24/7, multilingual (English, Chinese, Korean). Illness, accidents, and general emergencies during your trip

🚒 There's a fire in the building next door. You dial 119 and the operator answers in Japanese.

What key information should you communicate first?

✅ The right move: Whether you need FIRE or AMBULANCE, then the location

Why: 119 covers both fire and ambulance, so operators first need to know which one — 'Kaji' (fire) or 'Kyūkyū' (ambulance) — and where. Speak slowly; many dispatch centers can arrange interpretation. Location details like a nearby landmark help crews find you.

Source: Fire and Disaster Management Agency (FDMA)

⛴️ During a ferry trip between islands, you witness a boating accident.

Which number reports emergencies at sea in Japan?

✅ The right move: 118

Why: 118 is the Japan Coast Guard's emergency number for reporting incidents and accidents at sea — most first reports of maritime accidents come through it. Worried about language? State the location slowly in simple words — and the 24/7 multilingual Japan Visitor Hotline (050-3816-2787) can help when you can't get through in Japanese. On land, remember 110 (police) and 119 (fire/ambulance).

Source: Japan Coast Guard — Saving lives
